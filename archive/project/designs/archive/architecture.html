System architecture · v2

Finding every entity in unstructured claim notes

A four-layer pipeline that reads every character of a claim file, resolves who is who across the entire corpus, and stores the result so any downstream system can ask questions of it.

01 The whole pipeline

Text enters on the left as raw notes and leaves on the right as a queryable entity graph. Each layer hands the next a strictly more structured artifact, and every artifact keeps a pointer back to the exact characters it came from.

02 Why three extractors, not one

A single LLM pass drifts. It reliably captures the main narrative and quietly drops the long tail — the paralegal named once in a signature block, the clinic in a footer, a lone diagnostic code. Those omissions are silent: nothing in the output says an entity was missed.

So three independent readers scan every chunk and their outputs are unioned, with each surviving span recording which readers saw it. A token-level NER model reads every literal token regardless of salience. Deterministic gazetteers with checksum validation catch structured codes — an LLM is never asked to find an NPI. The LLM contributes what only it can: context, roles, and relationships.

On the current fixture this is the largest single effect in the pipeline. Adding the token-level reader moved entity recall by more than twenty points; adding gazetteers moved structured-identifier recall from near-nothing to complete.

03 Identity is a view, not a merge

The conventional move is to decide once that two mentions are the same person and write that decision into the store. It is also the move that makes a graph quietly wrong: one bad merge is indistinguishable from a true one afterwards, and one bad veto silently splits a person forever.

Instead, resolution (Splink, Fellegi–Sunter with EM-calibrated weights) emits SAME_AS edges carrying a real probability, and identity is computed from them at a chosen threshold. Raising the threshold splits; lowering it merges. Nothing is destroyed, so a questionable link is a low-weight edge you can filter — not a structural mistake baked into the data.

This is not a theoretical nicety. On the rebuilt corpus the intuitive threshold of 0.90 yields precision 0.997 and recall 0.106 — a 16,766-entity graph where the truth is 570. The workable operating point, 0.45, was read off a measured curve rather than assumed. Under a destructive-merge design that first choice would have been written into the store with no way to see the mistake, let alone undo it.

04 Identifiers are entities too

A note that says a payment went to an address, and never says whose address, is worthless to a system that stores addresses as text on a person record. It is highly informative to one that stores the address as its own node.

This is where the rebuilt corpus paid for itself. Measured against it, recall on name-less identifier mentions was 0% — none of 1,211. The pattern matchers had found every one of them correctly; the pipeline then discarded any identifier it could not attach to a nearby name. The capability the system exists to provide was being silently thrown away, and the previous corpus contained no such mentions, so nothing could have revealed it. Identifiers are now recorded whether or not a name binds: 0% → 100%.

Addresses, phones, emails, TINs, NPIs and VINs are resolved and stored as first-class nodes with their own association history. That makes the unnamed mention a two-hop path instead of a dead end, and it makes the history queryable: who was reachable at this number, and when.

05 What each layer guarantees

The pipeline is designed so that every claim it makes is checkable against the text it came from, and so that no stage silently discards information the next stage might need.

  • Every span keeps its origin. Document id and character offsets travel with each mention, assertion and edge, so any fact in a dossier reopens the note at the exact characters that assert it.
  • Nothing is dropped for not fitting a schema. Relationship predicates are open vocabulary and normalized toward canonical forms over time, rather than filtered against a fixed list at write time.
  • Scope is a filter, not a wall. Retrieval can restrict to one claim; identity never does. A person is the same person across every claim and occurrence in the corpus.
  • Confidence is carried, not hidden. Every association and every identity link stores a probability, so downstream systems can choose their own operating point.

What is not good enough yet

  • Resolution is still roughly twice as fragmented as the truth — 1,010 entities where there are 570. The measured cause is candidate generation: 27% of genuinely co-referring pairs are never compared at all, which caps how much recall any scoring model can recover.
  • Coreference is the weakest link at 43%. Counter-intuitively it is worse on direct references than on chained ones, because the nearest preceding person in dense prose is frequently not the referent — and a nearest-mention rule takes it anyway. This is the component most likely to improve with a real model rather than more tuning.
  • Bare single-token names are missed outright — a lone surname is rejected by the name-shape filter. A deliberate trade, now measured, and the clearest next lever.
  • Event extraction is not built. The ground truth for it exists and is waiting.
