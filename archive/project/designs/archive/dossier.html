Three things in that view are not obtainable from the notes today. The alias set is evidence that five different spellings were resolved to one person. The closed phone window means a later mention of the old number will not be wrongly attributed to her. And the second evidence item is attributed to her even though the note names nobody — the identifier node carries it.

Allegations never become facts. The questioned billing appears on the timeline and in evidence, marked and separated. The system records that someone alleged it, with the span that proves they did — it does not assert it.

02 Enriching the claim assistant

The assistant already filters to a claim and retrieves the most relevant chunks. That gate stays exactly as it is. The entity layer is consulted after it — on the way out, not instead of it.

Retrieved chunks contain entity mentions. Those mentions are already resolved to global identities, so the assistant can attach what is known about each party from the whole corpus before it composes an answer — and can answer questions that are structurally impossible for claim-scoped retrieval alone.

03 The resolution layer as a shared service

Nothing in the extraction and resolution core is specific to one team's corpus. It takes unstructured text and returns resolved entities, links and provenance — which makes it usable by any group with a document pile and a need to know who is in it.

Each consuming team keeps its own corpus and its own graph. What they share is the engine: the same extraction ensemble, the same resolution model, the same evidence discipline. On top of a team's graph sits a chat interface that answers over their entities rather than over raw text alone.

The rebuild earned its keep immediately. Against the realistic corpus, identifiers mentioned without a name were being captured correctly and then silently discarded — none of 1,211 survived. That is precisely the "address with no name" case this system exists to solve, and the earlier simplified corpus contained no such mentions, so nothing could have surfaced it. Now fully recovered.

05 What this is worth

  • A question that is impossible today becomes routine. Whether a party recurs across claims cannot be answered by claim-scoped retrieval at any level of model quality. It is an architectural limit, and the entity layer is what removes it.
  • Every answer carries its receipt. Each fact reopens the note at the characters that assert it, which is what makes the output usable where decisions have to be defended.
  • Confidence is explicit. Links are scored rather than asserted, so a reviewer can see the difference between a validated identifier match and a co-occurrence pattern.
  • The capability is reusable. Nothing in the core assumes claims — any team with documents and parties can send text in and get resolved entities back.

The dossier above is a mockup using a fictional entity, illustrating the target output. Underlying performance is now measured against a corpus rebuilt to match production note characteristics: identifier capture is complete (including identifiers mentioned with no name attached), entity capture runs in the mid-eighties percent, and identity resolution is roughly twice as fragmented as the truth — good enough to demonstrate the capability, not yet good enough to ship. Coreference is the weakest component and the current focus.
