Six weeks, dependency-scheduled

Every bar's position was computed from the actual dependency graph in the ticket plan, not eyeballed against the sprint boundaries — resource-constrained scheduling, one lane = one person working sequentially. Two things fell out of doing that honestly, both worth knowing before this goes to the client:

Finding 1 — DS1 alone doesn't fit in six weeks

If DS1 owns the entire corpus/locator/sampling epic (A→B→C) serially and only then does the freeze (F3→H1→H2), that lane alone runs to week 8, not week 6 — the honest total is ~33 person-days of serial work against a 30-day budget. Meanwhile DS2 finishes the tooling epic (E1–E5) by week 2–3 and then sits idle for the rest of the project under the original split.

Fix applied below: once DS2 clears the tooling epic, the identifier locator (B3), the not-found bucket (B5), and the sampling epic (C1–C3) hand off to DS2. Same total work, redistributed — this is what actually gets the plan back to six weeks, and it's the reason this chart exists rather than the flat ticket list.

Finding 2 — a missing dependency in the original ticket table

F2 (adjudication) was written as depending only on F1 (gold annotation producing conflicts to adjudicate) — but adjudication also needs the adjudication queue, E4, to exist. That dependency was missing from the plan. It's added below; it does not change the critical path (E4 already finishes well before F1 does), but it would have been a real gap if someone scheduled off the table as written.
